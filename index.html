Wheel-legged robots and embedded MPC

2023–present

I have worked on the control and embedded software of T-DT’s omnidirectional infantry, serial-leg infantry, and serial-leg hero robots. For the wheel-legged platforms, I developed balance controllers and leg-length-dependent models, and implemented an ADMM-based MPC solver on STM32H7, following TinyMPC.

Code: MPC and collision observer

Implementation notes

The deployed controller uses a prediction horizon of 8, with 10 states and 4 control inputs. On a 550 MHz STM32H7, one solve takes no more than 4 ms. The model uses CAD mass and inertia parameters; state-space matrices and solver caches are fitted offline across leg lengths, then updated online. This controller has been deployed on our 2026 serial-leg hero robot.

The repository also includes a five-DoF generalized momentum observer, collision-detection logic, and symbolic M/C/G derivation. It contains only these modules, not the full robot firmware. The numerical model and observer have known limitations; a residual crossing a threshold does not by itself confirm a collision. See the repository README for the test scope and caveats.

Language robustness in SmolVLA

Aug. 2026–present

I study whether a VLA policy behaves consistently when an instruction is rephrased—for example, replacing “next to” with “beside.” I built training and paired-evaluation pipelines using LeRobot, SmolVLA, and LIBERO Spatial, and tested language augmentation and instruction normalization.

Evaluation setup and preliminary results

I compare correct, empty, shuffled, and mapped instructions while fixing the initial state and the Flow Matching noise for each action chunk. Under matched images, states, and noise, I inspect the VLM’s K/V representations, the Action Expert’s velocity predictions, and the resulting action chunks.

In a small experiment with 432 demonstrations and 1,000 fine-tuning steps, success under synonymous instructions increased from 2/30 to 6/30 trials. In a separate set of 21 known-task pairs, instruction normalization with a frozen policy increased successes from 1/21 to 9/21. These are preliminary results for those specific settings, not general benchmark scores.

Imitation learning and ACT in LIBERO

Sept. 2026–present

I built a reproducible single-task imitation-learning pipeline in LIBERO Spatial, implementing State BC, dual-camera Vision BC, RNN BC, Transformer BC, and Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT). The project covers HDF5 observation-action alignment, spatial visual features, sequence modeling, CVAE-style latent variables, action chunks, temporal ensembling, and closed-loop evaluation.

Code and reproducibility materials · Experiment report · 20-second video summary

Protocol, results, and scope

All available checkpoints were evaluated on the same ten initial states, with the same stabilization procedure and a 220-step limit. State BC, RNN BC, Transformer BC, and ACT achieved 10/10, 9/10, 0/10, and 9/10 successes respectively. ACT had the shortest mean successful trajectory at 92.7 steps.

The experiment shows why low offline validation error is not sufficient evidence of closed-loop robot performance: the Transformer baseline had validation error comparable to the successful baselines but failed every rollout. These results cover one task, ten rollouts, and one random seed; they are a reproducible systems comparison, not a general model ranking.

Sim-to-real locomotion for a serial-leg robot

Mar. 2026–present

I am building a training and deployment pipeline in Isaac Lab with rsl_rl. The policy uses PPO, an LSTM, and proprioceptive observations for velocity tracking and balance on flat ground, slopes, and steps.

Training and deployment

My work includes observation and reward design, policy structure, and deployment. I track falls and velocity-following behavior, and investigate how differences between the simulated model and the physical robot affect the policy.

In the reported real-robot tests, the flat-ground fall rate decreased from nearly 100% to a single-digit percentage, and the blind policy crossed an 8 cm step in approximately 84% of trials.

Code as Policies for dynamic manipulation

Aug. 2026–present

I run local Code as Policies experiments for dynamic visual sorting, using Trossen and MuJoCo backends. I integrated skills, task and motion planning (TAMP), and a robot API into a shared evaluation setup for three agent variants.

Experiment setup and results

The task requires three cubes to be placed into numbered bins whose visible labels change after each successful release. A live policy virtual machine allows generated Python control flow to observe the new physical state between macro actions, while TAMP handles reachability, inverse kinematics, and safe trajectories.

Across seeds 411–413, all three local systems eventually completed the three-decision task. The CaP runtime completed every trial with its first policy, using one planning call and 34.3 primitive actions on average; the two static-plan adapters required three planning rounds and 68.7 actions. The HoloAgent-0 and PhyAgentOS variants are local architecture adapters, not evaluations of their official learned policies or hardware stacks.
